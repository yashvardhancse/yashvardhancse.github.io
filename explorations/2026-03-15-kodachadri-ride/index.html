Sea and mountains in one day

My friends took the car. I took the Pulsar and the long way — up the coast past Maravanthe, then into the Western Ghats to Kodachadri.

Mar 15, 2026 3 min read By YashVardhan Gupta

Some trips you plan for weeks. This one came together in a night: a few friends wanted to see Kodachadri, one of the taller peaks in the Western Ghats. They were taking a car. I wanted the ride — so I took my Pulsar, and we agreed to meet at the top.

The deal I made with myself was to go the long way: up the coast first, so I could put the sea and the mountains in the same day.

I left Manipal early and rode up the coast to Maravanthe, where the highway runs on a thin strip of land with the Arabian Sea on one side. I stopped while the light was still soft, killed the engine, and listened to the water for a while. There is something about a cold start, an empty beach and a full tank that sets the tone for the whole day.

Turning inland

From the coast the road bends east and begins to climb. The scenery changes fast: within an hour the open sea gives way to dry March forest, bare trees and red-earth shoulders. Traffic thins to almost nothing — long patched stretches where it is just the bike, the road and the sound of the engine.

Into the hills

Closer to Kodachadri the hills stack up ahead of you. This is the part I ride for: a narrow road threading between green slopes, the needle holding steady, nowhere in particular to be. I stopped more often than I needed to, just to look.

The top

Riding solo and meeting friends who drove turned out to be the best of both: hours of quiet on the way up, and people to share the view with once I got there. We walked the last stretch to the grass ridge at the top and stood looking out over range after range of the Ghats fading into haze.

Sea in the morning, mountains by the afternoon — all on one tank of fuel. That is the whole reason I keep riding.
