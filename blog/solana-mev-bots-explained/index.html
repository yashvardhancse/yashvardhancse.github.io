Solana MEV Bots Explained: How They Work & What They Earn

Reports show some operators made far more than $50,000 a day at the 2024-25 peak, but no public data shows that is typical. Here is how searchers, Jito bundles and tips work, what the data says bots earn, and how the "$50k a day" scams operate.

Key takeaways

  • No public data shows $50,000 a day is typical for a Solana MEV bot, though Helius reported far more for one sandwich program over 30 days at the 2024-25 peak. Everyone who paid Jito tips spent about $193,000 a day in the 30 days to 28 October 2026.[1]
  • Solana has no public mempool. Many searchers send bundles through Jito's Block Engine; others send ordinary transactions with priority fees, which still cost a fee when they fail.
  • Profit per opportunity is small: in this article's simulation a 2% gap between two 10,000 SOL pools earns about $41.51 after pool fees, before the tip.
  • A daily-income MEV bot for sale matches the standard scam lure, and fake bot packages are built to steal private keys.[2] Never paste a key into code you did not write and read.

A Solana MEV bot is an automated program, called a searcher, that watches on-chain prices and sends transactions or Jito bundles with a tip to capture profit from the order of transactions in a block. Arbitrage and liquidation bots keep prices aligned and lenders repaid; sandwich bots, which profit at users' expense, do the clearest harm.

Can a Solana MEV bot really make $50,000 a day?

Not as a normal outcome, though some operators did far more at the 2024-25 peak. Helius reported one sandwich program's profit at roughly $448,000 a day for 30 days, taken from other users (net of tips not stated), when the daily tip pool was more than 20 times larger in dollars.[3][1] No public dataset reports what a typical bot earns, and today's aggregates make $50,000 a day unlikely for one.

DefiLlama counted about $193,000 a day in tips from everyone who paid Jito over the 30 days to 28 October 2026.[1] A bot netting $50,000 a day after tips and fees, but before servers, capital and developer time, would need roughly 5% to 60% of that, depending on its tip share.

Tips are a cost, and the pool counts Jito tips only

DefiLlama's series counts only tips paid to Jito's tip accounts. It leaves out priority fees (DefiLlama counts $23.8 million of base and priority fees paid by Solana users over the same 30 days[1]), tips to other landing services (51% of tip volume in March 2026, per Syndica) and private deals.[4]

The $50,000 arithmetic, as scenarios

Say a bot pays a share s of its pre-tip profit as tips. To net $50,000 it must pay $50,000 × s / (1 − s) in tips daily. Published shares are sparse: about 5% of the gain in The Block's back-run (below), and 26% to 35% of profit for the Helius sandwich program, depending on whether its profit is before or after tips.[5][3]

A second test avoids tips: Bitquery put all sandwich takings at $29.7 million from 30 October 2025 to 7 October 2026, about $87,000 a day before costs, so one bot netting $50,000 would need over half.[6] In January 2025 the same $50,000 was 0.5% of the average day's tips.[1] The pool has since fallen 49-fold in dollars but about 24-fold in SOL, because SOL's price roughly halved.

The Block reported a January 2024 back-run that turned 703 SOL into 19,035 SOL in about 20 seconds, caused by another trader's mistake.[5] Neither it nor the sandwich program Helius reported is a typical daily income.

What is MEV on Solana, and how does it differ from Ethereum?

MEV stands for maximal extractable value: the extra value someone can capture by including, excluding or reordering transactions in a block, beyond the block reward and fees.[7] On Solana the validator producing the current block, the slot leader, controls the order, and searchers compete for good positions in it.

Ethereum builders assemble whole blocks, while on Solana Jito's Block Engine auctions bundles and the leader builds the rest.[8] Ethereum also has a public mempool and Solana's design has none.[9]

Why does Solana have no public mempool?

Because Solana forwards transactions instead of pooling them. A mempool is a shared waiting room for pending transactions; Solana's Gulf Stream design sends each one toward the upcoming leaders, so there is no queue to read or reorder.[10] As of 29 October 2026, SIMD-0525, a Solana Improvement Document from Anza, is Activated and sets a 200 ms target slot time, down from 400 ms, and a leader gets four slots in a row, about 800 ms.[11]

Jito once added something close to a public queue: from 2022 until Jito Labs ended it over sandwich attacks in March 2024, its relayer held transactions for about 200 ms so searchers connected to the Block Engine could bid to trade around them.[3][9] A 2025 study notes speculation that some validators re-created a mempool privately.[9]

How do Jito bundles and tips work?

A bundle is a group of up to five transactions meant to run in the listed order, inside one slot, all succeeding or none committed. A tip is a SOL transfer to one of eight Jito tip accounts, at least 1,000 lamports (a lamport is a billionth of a SOL), and it is how a bundle bids for inclusion.[8]

Many searchers send bundles to a Block Engine, Jito's auction service. Auctions run every 50 ms and the highest-paying combination goes to the leader. According to Jito, only leaders running its validator software can include bundles, and validators and their stakers share the tips.[8] Others send ordinary transactions with priority fees.[8] Block building is diversifying: in March 2026 Syndica found 28% of stake on JitoBAM, a Jito extension, and 17% on Harmonic, where independent builders compete.[4]

Solana charges fees whether or not a transaction succeeds: 5,000 lamports per signature, half burned and half to the validator, plus an optional priority fee, paid entirely to the validator, equal to a price per compute unit (a measure of work) times a limit.[12]

A bundle is different: Jito says that if any transaction in a bundle fails, none is committed, and advises putting the tip inside the strategy transaction so a failed transaction pays no tip.[8] It does not state what an unlanded bundle costs, and it warns that after a skipped (uncled) block others can rebroadcast a bundle's transactions individually, outside the all-or-nothing protection, where they can land and fail.

What strategies do Solana MEV bots use?

Four families dominate the evidence, and only the sandwich is built to worsen a user's price on their own swap.

A mispriced pool is a market maker quoting an old price, so arbitrage corrects it at liquidity providers' expense, the adverse selection the guide to market making explains. The 2026 bot study also counts token-launch sniping, buying new tokens within seconds, as a Solana MEV family.[13]

How does a Solana arbitrage bot work? A Python simulation

A 2026 study of Solana bot code found a shared five-stage pipeline from setup through observation, analytics and planning to execution.[13] In practice a searcher streams chain data, prices routes in memory, simulates a trade, adds a compute budget and tip, sends it to a regional Jito Block Engine and checks whether it landed, weighing latency at every step.[8]

The simplest pool design, and the one modelled here, uses the constant-product rule x × y = k, so a bigger trade moves the price further. That move is slippage: the gap between the expected price and the average price you got. The script uses made-up pools and only Python's standard library, with no network, wallet or randomness. Its best_size function finds the best trade in closed form and a brute-force scan in 10 USDC steps checks it; the tip is an assumption, half the gross profit.

In scenario 1 the pools differ by 2.00% and charge 0.25% per side. The best input is 5,590.2 USDC and gross profit is 41.510 USDC. The trade shrinks the gap to 0.500%, about twice the fee. Profit falls to zero near 11,222, twice the best size. These results hold only for constant-product pools with a flat fee.

So the 2.00% gap becomes a 0.74% return on the 5,590 USDC traded. Network fees are noise at 0.00525 USDC, and the assumed tip halves what remains, to 20.750. A 0.80% gap nets 0.822, so $50,000 a day would take 2,410 or 60,862 identical winning trades (from unrounded nets). Real markets add rivals who bid the tip toward the whole edge, failed attempts and inventory risk.

What does it cost to run a Solana MEV bot, and who actually earns?

Tips, failed attempts and infrastructure decide the outcome, and none is published for a typical bot. In a sample of 53 days between August 2023 and July 2024, 1.51 billion of 2.90 billion non-vote transactions failed (52.1%); accounts classified as bots failed 58.43% of the time, against 6.22% for human-classified accounts.[14]

Helius's pricing page, viewed on 29 October 2026, lists a streaming-data plan from $499 a month and dedicated nodes from $2,900; competitive setups are not priced publicly.[15] Capital, engineering time and landing rates have no public data, and a 2026 study of 200 top-ranked addresses from two bot services found, over October 2025, that trading frequency and success rate did not reliably translate into stable profit.[13]

The two sandwich averages are not comparable: one covers three years and only persistent bots, which flatters it, the other 11 months and every sandwich Bitquery detected. Both are this article's own division of reported totals.[16][6]

Averages hide a heavy tail. Helius reported that Jito's analysis attributed nearly half of all Solana sandwich attacks to one program.[3] In a 63-attack Bitquery sample, which does not establish net earnings for the full period, bots kept 68% of gross proceeds and 18 attacks lost money after costs.[6]

How do you protect yourself from sandwich attacks on Solana?

Choose a deliberate slippage setting, use protected submission where it is offered, and be careful with tokens on Pump.fun, a meme-coin launchpad, and PumpSwap, its exchange, which were involved in 96% of the attacks Bitquery detected; none of this is a guarantee. A peer-reviewed study of four months of Jito data in early 2025 found 521,903 sandwiches and at least $7.7 million in victim losses (at the study's September 2025 SOL price of about $242), with a median loss near $5.[9]

  1. Set slippage deliberately. Tolerance caps how far below the quote you accept: at 1%, a $1,000 swap can lose at most about $10 to price movement of any kind, and at 20% about $200. In Bitquery's observational data, 8 in 10,000 Jupiter (a swap aggregator) swaps through Pump.fun or PumpSwap at 1% to 3% tolerance were sandwiched, against 148 at 20% to 50%, but the pattern is not smooth: swaps under 0.5% were hit 27 times, and exact-quote swaps 36.[6] Setting it too tight can make your own swap fail, and failed transactions still pay fees.
  2. Use protected submission. Jito's sendTransaction adds MEV protection by default, and bundleOnly=true sends a single-transaction bundle.[8] Jupiter's protection option produced such bundles in the 2025 study, where users spent about $2.4 million (same SOL price) on defensive tips.[9]
  3. Do not rely on jitodontfront. An account address with that prefix makes the Block Engine reject a bundle unless the transaction comes first, and Jito says it is not guaranteed.[8] A 2026 preprint found it covers only ordering inside a bundle: one large trading app had set it on 98.5% of its 21.5 million sandwiched victim transactions.[16]

Are Solana MEV bots legal?

Rules differ by country and this is not legal advice. ESMA, the EU markets regulator, notes that MEV is not legally defined and has seen very few enforcement actions, which is not the same as lawful. Its July 2025 analysis says some MEV may be market abuse and needs further study.[17]

ESMA described a US prosecution opened in May 2024 as a first-of-its-kind case involving MEV; it concerns Ethereum.[17] Prosecutors allege two brothers tricked other traders' MEV bots out of about $25 million, a case not about sandwiching users. A trial ended in a mistrial in November 2025. The latest report available, from March 2026, said the judge would rule on a motion to dismiss before any retrial, so check the docket (United States v. Peraire-Bueno, SDNY 24-cr-293).[18]

How do "$X a day" Solana MEV bot scams work?

The pitch typically promises a fixed daily or monthly income, then asks for a wallet connection, a deposit, a private key or a fee for a "package".

  • Deploy-and-fund contracts (Ethereum). SentinelLABS described YouTube videos pushing arbitrage-bot contracts, which the videos call MEV bots, that viewers deploy and fund with at least 0.5 ETH. The contract secretly lets the scammer withdraw the funds. One wallet tied to a single video, a clear outlier, received 244.9 ETH (about $902,000).[19]
  • Key-stealing packages (Solana). JFrog's June 2026 report on malicious npm and PyPI packages includes one named solana-mev-bot, presented as a profit-generating MEV bot, that asks users to paste their private key and searches the machine for other credentials. JFrog does not say whether anyone lost money.[2]

Red flags

  • A promised daily or monthly return, or a success rate near 100%, the core of the AI trading-bot frauds the US CFTC warned about in January 2024.[20]
  • A request to deploy, fund and "activate" a bot contract,[19] connect a wallet, or pay a fee to withdraw.
  • Closed or obfuscated code, pushed through videos, social posts or chat groups.[19][20]
  • Proof offered only as screenshots and testimonials, or an anonymous author or newly registered domain.[20]

Safe handling

  1. Never hand over your main wallet. No website, chat or support agent needs your seed phrase. A bot you run yourself needs a signing key, so give it a separate wallet, never your savings or an exchange-linked one.
  2. Start with no real value, and read the code. Use devnet and test tokens, or a model like the one above. Check dependencies, pin versions and run unfamiliar code only in a virtual machine that holds none of your keys or credentials. Fund a fresh wallet with an amount you can afford to lose, and only for code you have read.
  3. Expect no guarantee. No legitimate searcher can promise a daily return. Report fraud to your national regulator or cybercrime portal; in the US, the CFTC at cftc.gov/complaint or the FBI at ic3.gov.[20]

Warning: If you already ran such code

Treat every key and credential on that machine as compromised, including those in .env files. From a different, clean device, create a new wallet with a new seed phrase, ideally a hardware wallet, and move your funds there. Then revoke token delegations, rotate SSH, API and cloud credentials, and wipe or reinstall the machine. If you funded a contract, stop sending funds and revoke its permissions.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Solana MEV bot really make $50,000 a day?

Not as a normal outcome, though some operators did far more at the 2024-25 peak: Helius reported about $13.4 million of profit in 30 days (roughly $448,000 a day, not stated to be net of tips) for one sandwich program, when the daily tip pool was more than 20 times bigger in dollar terms (about 12 times in SOL). No public dataset reports what a typical bot earns, and in the 30 days to 28 October 2026 everyone who paid Jito tips (bots, apps and ordinary users) spent only about $193,000 a day in total, a cost to bots rather than their profit, so $50,000 a day is unlikely for one bot. An offer that promises a daily income matches the standard scam lure.

How much do Solana MEV bots make?

No public dataset reports what a typical bot earns. The averages that exist are small per opportunity: about $1.58 profit per detected arbitrage in 2024 (Helius) and about $12 net per sandwich as a mean for persistent, mostly profitable bots over July 2023 to June 2026 (a 2026 preprint, dividing its reported totals). Activity is concentrated, with nearly half of all sandwich attacks attributed to one program in Helius's report, so treat any single "top bot" figure as a best case, not an average.

Does Solana have a mempool?

Not in the protocol. Transactions are forwarded toward the upcoming leaders instead of waiting in a shared public pool. From 2022 until Jito Labs suspended it in March 2024 after sandwich attacks, Jito's relayer held incoming transactions for about 200 ms so that searchers could bid on them. A 2025 study notes speculation that some validators re-created a mempool privately.

What is a Jito bundle and what is a Jito tip?

A bundle is up to five transactions that are meant to run in the order listed, in one slot, all or nothing. A tip is a SOL transfer of at least 1,000 lamports to one of eight Jito tip accounts, and bundles bid with tips in auctions run every 50 ms. Only leaders running Jito's validator software can include bundles, and Jito warns that after a skipped block a bundle's transactions can be rebroadcast individually, outside the all-or-nothing protection, and may land and fail. Searchers can also send ordinary transactions with priority fees, which still cost a fee when they fail.

How do I avoid sandwich attacks on Solana?

Use a deliberate slippage setting, use protected submission where your wallet or aggregator offers it, and be careful with tokens traded on Pump.fun or PumpSwap, which were involved in 96% of the attacks Bitquery detected (30 October 2025 to 7 October 2026). Bitquery's observational data showed far fewer sandwiched swaps at 1% to 3% slippage than at 20% to 50%, yet the pattern was not smooth and even exact-quote swaps were sandwiched, so these steps reduce the risk without removing it. A setting that is too tight can make your own swap fail, and failed transactions still pay fees.

Are Solana MEV bots legal?

Rules differ by country and this is not legal advice. ESMA notes that MEV is not legally defined and has seen very few enforcement actions, which is not the same as lawful: its July 2025 analysis says some MEV may be market abuse and needs further study. The best-known US criminal case concerns an alleged Ethereum exploit of other traders' bots, not sandwiching users. Profits may also be taxable, with rules that vary by country.

Is a free or paid Solana MEV bot from GitHub, YouTube or Telegram safe?

Treat it as unsafe until proven otherwise. Security firms have documented a malicious npm package named solana-mev-bot that asks for a private key and searches for other credentials (JFrog, June 2026) and YouTube tutorials that lead victims to fund a contract co-owned by the scammer (SentinelLABS, on Ethereum). Never paste a private key or seed phrase into code you did not write and read. If you already ran such code, treat every key on that machine as compromised and move your funds to a new wallet created on a different, clean device.

Why do so many Solana transactions fail?

Fees are low, so bots send many attempts when only one can win an opportunity. A study of 53 sampled days between August 2023 and July 2024 found that 1.51 billion of 2.9 billion non-vote transactions failed (52.1%), with bot-classified accounts failing 58.43% of the time and human-classified accounts 6.22%. A verified 2026 failure rate was not found.
